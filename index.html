Profile

Engineering small systems for large diagnostic questions.

The work sits where physical devices meet physiological uncertainty: soft adhesion, magnetic navigation, sensing, computation, and the messy grace of real-world constraints.

I’m a master’s student in Robotics, Systems and Control at ETH Zürich, where I develop robots for in vivo diagnosis at the Multi-Scale Robotics Lab advised by Professor Bradley Nelson. Before ETH, I completed my B.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering with distinction at RWTH Aachen University.

I’m interested in intelligent medical devices that make diagnosis and therapy more precise, less invasive, and easier to deploy. My work spans in-body medical robots, bioadhesive sensing patches, 3D electronics and embedded systems, and multi-scale computational mechanics from continuum to atomistic levels.

Outside the lab, I cycle through Switzerland on a Canyon Aeroad and listen to classical music, especially Bach and Rachmaninoff.

Development of a simulation module for sub-Kolmogorov deformable droplets coupled with an in-house lattice Boltzmann solver.

Atomistic simulation of a medium-entropy alloy with a gradient nano-grained structure under cryogenic-temperature tensile deformation.

Abaqus models with custom UMAT scripts for flash and ultra-high-speed sintering, calibrated against experimental data.
